The Experimentation Journal

Day 1: 8:00 PM

It's been a week since the incident at the circus. My beloved child, once full of life, now lies in bed, her health deteriorating rapidly. The doctors diagnosed a malignant tumor in her frontal lobe. Conventional treatments are proving too slow, and her vision is fading. Every moment, I watch helplessly as she suffers more. I can't bear to see her like this. I must find a way to save her, no matter the cost. There has to be something more I can do.


Day 7: 11:30 AM

Traditional medicine has failed us. Her condition worsens daily, and I'm running out of time. Today, I began exploring alternative methods. I came across Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT) as a potential option. It sounds extreme, but I’m desperate. I administered controlled electric shocks to her brain, hoping to reduce the tumor's size. The pain she endured was excruciating, and it broke my heart to see her suffer, but I have to believe this is the right path. I stayed by her side, holding her hand, whispering reassurances. I hope this works.


Day 14: 2:15 PM

The ECT showed little improvement. I cannot stop now. Radiation therapy is my next course of action. Today, I increased the dosage beyond what is medically advised, targeting the tumor with high-intensity radiation. Each session leaves her weaker, but the tumor must shrink. The doctors warned me of the risks, but what choice do I have? I see the fatigue in her eyes, and it kills me. I keep telling her to stay strong, that we will get through this, but I wonder if I’m trying to convince myself more than her.


Day 21: 10:00 PM

The tumor resists all efforts. Nothing seems to work. Today, I turned to experimental chemicals, injecting untested substances directly into her system. These chemicals, though dangerous, might be our last hope. Her body reacted violently, convulsing and trembling. The guilt gnaws at me, but my determination to save her remains unshaken. I keep telling myself that these are the steps we need to take, that it’s worth it if it means saving her life. But seeing her in so much pain makes me question everything.


Day 28: 5:45 AM

Weeks have passed, and the toll of these treatments is evident. She has grown weaker, both physically and mentally. She talks less, often staring blankly at the ceiling. Her once-bright eyes are now clouded with confusion and pain. I see the suffering I've caused, but I can't stop. Every moment is a reminder of my failure to protect her from the initial incident. I sit by her bed, holding her hand, praying for a miracle. I don’t know how much more she can take, but I can’t lose hope. Not yet.


Day 35: 3:30 PM

Her mental state is deteriorating rapidly. She speaks of voices, dark and whispering, that haunt her every waking moment. I fear my efforts to save her have driven her to the brink of madness. The chemicals, the radiation, the electric shocks – they've all taken their toll. I feel the weight of my decisions crushing my spirit, but I can't let her go. I’m terrified that my actions have caused irreversible damage. The guilt is unbearable, but I have to keep going. There’s no turning back now.


Day 42: 9:00 PM

It's been months since I started this journey. The tumor has receded slightly, but at what cost? Her mind is fractured, her body frail. I am left to ponder the ethical boundaries I crossed. The once clear line between right and wrong is now blurred. I wanted to save her, but have I only prolonged her suffering? I spend sleepless nights replaying every decision, every treatment. I wanted to be her savior, but I fear I’ve become her . The weight of my actions is crushing me.


Day 49: 6:15 AM

I look back on these months with a heavy heart. My relentless pursuit of a cure has caused irreparable damage. The pain and trauma she endured under my care haunt me. This journal, filled with my desperate attempts, stands as a testament to a father's love and the lengths he will go to save his child. But it also serves as a warning. In my quest to save her, I lost sight of the ethical lines that should never be crossed. I wonder if I’ll ever forgive myself for what I’ve done.


Day 56: 8:45 PM

As I write this final entry, I realize the gravity of my actions. This journey has not only been about trying to save my child but also about understanding the consequences of desperation. I document these experiences not for forgiveness, but to serve as a cautionary tale. Let my story be a lesson to others about the dangers of unbridled desperation and the ethical dilemmas faced in the pursuit of saving a loved one. I hope that in sharing this, I can prevent someone else from making the same mistakes. My love for her blinded me, and now I must live with the consequences.
